*TW: traumatic labour and delivery, specific description of experiencing postpartum depression*

The day my baby was born, I lost my mind.

It happened when I tried to breastfeed and nothing came. It was just after I gave birth. The nurses and doctors had finally left. We were still in the birthing suite; I was surrounded by a village. I was alone. I was incapable.

I used up everything to give birth. I was a hollowed husk and I had nothing left to give her.

After my water broke and the contractions started, I was curious to know what it felt like. An hour later, I relearned the expression curiosity killed the cat. That was when I got the first epidural.

We tried to sleep to recover our strength before active labour. Zander woke to me humming, low and demonic.

The nurses came back to check on me. They pressed an ice pack to my arm. They pressed it to my leg. They asked what that felt like. Ice cold I said.

Ice cold I kept saying over and over again. Ice cold I said as they punctured my spine a second and third time. I was contracting more than I wasn't by the second and third time. They told me I had to hold perfectly still through the contractions to make sure they didn't damage my spine.

They had Zander seated in front of me. I bend forward, clenching Zander hard enough to leave marks. My shoulders kept creeping upward as they told me to try to relax them.

There's never been a less relaxing situation.

I begged for a c-section. They said it was too late for that, she was already almost here. I begged to stop. I said I could not do it anymore. I said I was giving up.

I told him to go to her, he should at least get to be with the baby. I thought that she was better off without me because I tried to give up on her. He was the one with the strength to pull me through the labour when I gave up. He held my hand and told me I had to keep going.

I had let go of his hand. I grabbed his pants so hard I yanked a fitful of his pubic hair as I pushed. A few inches over and a second baby would be impossible. A small petty revenge for everyone that told me as I was going through the pain of labour that it would all be easier when I had my second baby.

My breath finally held tight, lifting my own head, pulling legs up to my chest as they had been firmly telling me to. Instructions I had been resisting for until that moment when my body took over and suddenly knew what to do. Zander told me later that they could tell that I was just pretending to push until that moment. I laughed and said that I tried to trick them, but I wasn't very sneaky. I wanted them to think I was doing it so they would stop ordering me around.

Zander overheard a nurse saying I lost twice as much blood as an average c-section. By the time I came to, most of the blood had been clean up except for one spot. It was the size and shape of a halved avocado, directly under the hospital bed.

Zander held my hand and between pushes I said I thought I was going to die. He held my hand and the doctor tugged at the placenta that remained stubbornly attached. He held my hand and an impossibly large wave of blood hit the ground. He held my hand and they rushed to fill my veins with medicines to staunch the bleeding.

Across the room, our purple baby surrounded by the NICU team rushing to clear her lungs. He held my hand and he wondered if he would lose us both that day.

After we were transferred to the Mother and Baby, I woke up to find her tucked under my arm with one breast exposed. I had absolutely no memory of how we had gotten there. It was confusing, frightening.

And it got worse.

I had been irresponsible. I wasn't practicing safe sleeping. I couldn't tell anyone because then they would know for sure I was unfit. They wouldn't let me keep her. Every single nurse or doctor that came in to see me heard me laughing and joking, then took my self compelled confession. I spilled and spilled to help them build a case to take her away because we couldn't keep her safe.

We were under surveillance. Every move we made was a tick in the unfit parent column and the moment we tried to leave the hospital an alarm would sound and jail bars would descend on the doors.

Everyone tried to reassure me. They don't take away  babies away for such trivial things. That frightened me more. Maybe they should. Maybe more babies should be protected from their unfit parents. If this wasn't enough then there was nothing keeping safe these helpless creatures that needed us and trusted us to keep them alive.

Later the nurse came in and confirm that we had had an entire conversation after her blood tests that I had no memory of. She placed Sunny in my arms to try to breastfeed again. She left pain medication for me to take afterwards.

Zander's body was making him pay the price too. We had not slept for over 24 hours. He had slept fitfully because he got up every few minutes to look for the baby in the nearby bassinet. It seemed like it had been too long since the nurse took her away for blood tests.

When I next tried to sleep, I felt my mind split into two. One side was exhausted and knew that Sunny was in safe arms with Zander; the other side was assessing every single sound I heard to determine whether he was making mistakes. Maybe I wasn't alone in fucking this whole thing up.  But he was perfect and I couldn't sleep.

And it got worse.

When I came to next, the mild contamination OCD that had always just been a small quirk became full blown delusions.

Everytime I touched her, I saw her warm glow turn radioactive. I tried to convince Zander that I was making dangerous mistakes. He swore he witnessed me do everything correctly. An hour after I almost bled out, I was walking around and making jokes. I told him I had managed to trick them all. I told him that he would leave me once he realized how much I had everyone fooled.

I clutched my contaminated hands to my chest. I waved them around to show how dirty they were. I tried to hit myself. I wanted to die. That was the only way to keep her safe. He held me down, compressing me like a cow in a shoot to try and calm me down. I had not been alone since I went into the hospital. I was convinced it was the only reason Sunny was till alive. I made him promise to never leave me alone with the baby. That deal was the only thing that got me to try sleeping again. I asked what if my mind never worked again.

I don’t remember if Sunny cried when her lungs finally cleared. It was an eternal instant. Zander told me afterward that the NICU team tried for a long time after she was breathing to get her to cry. They finally concluded that it was simply not her temperament to cry. But she was perfectly healthy.

I had apparently pushed her out in 17 minutes.

We were discharged after 2 nights at the hospital. I had been admitted for an induction on Monday morning, gave birth at 6am on Tuesday, and we discharged on Wednesday. The discharge papers noted that despite all of the complications, the recovery for both mother and baby was "unremarkable".

Everything went wrong and we survived.

In the last two weeks, as her wake windows have increased, she is wide eyed and hungry. She is taking in the whole wide world, shocked to be suddenly in it and amazed too.

She has old eyes like she’s been here before.She makes grunting and groaning sounds as she moves. An newborn old man grumbling about having a body. She flaps her arms like she’s an inflatable balloon man and we are perspective car buyers.

She still doesn’t cry. She’s so calm that she seems to be in a state of enlightenment beyond us.  We bother her regularly to make sure she’s breathing. I wonder out loud whether they’ve found the future Buddha yet.

Everything in her radius is bathed in a warm glow. A bubble of happiness that grabs hold of you and refuses to let go.

Based on Sunny's time of birth, my dad gave us three options for her Chinese names. The number of strokes in the characters corresponds with the weight of her spirit, her *chi*. We settle on one that means a clear day (*Ching*), for Sunny, and the Canadian maple tree, the kind whose leaves are carried away by the wind in autumn (*Feng*).

She blew into our lives like a whirlwind of autumn leaves, sweeping away the clouds. The skies have been clear, it's been an unseasonably warm November. My depression, anxiety, and postpartum depression scores plummeted after a week in her light.

It took 2 more days for my milk to finally come in. The first time she latched and swallowed milk, my entire body relaxed. My uterus contracted, a gush of afterbirth was released.

The fog cleared. The world became Sunny.
